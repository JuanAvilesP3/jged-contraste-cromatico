Dear Editor-in-Chief and Editorial Board of *JGED*,

We are pleased to submit our original research manuscript titled **"Chromatic Accessibility in Latin American University Websites: A Large-Scale Survey of WCAG Contrast Compliance"** for publication as a regular research article in the *Journal of Graphic Engineering and Design* (JGED). Our study directly addresses visual ergonomics and digital colorimetry, fitting JGED's core scope in graphic engineering and color science.

In this work, we report an empirical survey of chromatic accessibility in Latin American higher education. Using a custom automated crawler with a real browser rendering engine, we extracted **41,360 computed (post-CSS) text/background color pairs** from 259 university homepages across **17 countries**. Each color pair was mathematically transformed from device-dependent sRGB into the perceptual **CIELAB ($L^*a^*b^*$) color space** and evaluated against the ISO-standard WCAG 2.2 contrast thresholds, cross-validated with the axe-core accessibility engine.

Our statistical analysis reveals that **21.6% of all color pairs fail the WCAG AA minimum contrast ratio**, with **93.5% of institutional websites exhibiting at least one critical visual failure**. Notably, an unsupervised clustering analysis in the full CIELAB $L^*a^*b^*$ domain demonstrates that **80.0% of all visual failures are concentrated in near-neutral, low-chroma clusters** (specifically the classic "light gray text on white background" design pattern). This provides actionable colorimetric recommendations for digital typographers and graphic designers in institutional environments.
